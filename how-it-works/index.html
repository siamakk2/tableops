What happens in the video, step by step

The invoice gets photographed, not typed. A produce delivery arrives with a paper invoice. Instead of someone keying four line items into a spreadsheet at midnight — or, more often, dropping it in a box — the chef photographs it. ResBizAI reads the supplier, the date and every line: heavy cream 12 qt at $46.80, ribeye 20 lb at $289.00, lemons one case at $38.50, pecorino 5 lb at $64.25. Four line items go into inventory at the price that supplier actually charged.
Recipes connect dishes to ingredients. Carbonara is linked once to guanciale, eggs, pecorino and pasta, with the amounts a plate uses. That link is the whole mechanism. It only has to be made a single time per dish.
Selling depletes stock on its own. Three carbonara sell. Guanciale drops from 4.00 lb to 3.25 lb, eggs from 60 to 51. Nobody counted anything. Food cost moves from 30.0% to 28.4% on screen because the cream came in cheaper than the last delivery, and the number updates as the facts change rather than at month end.
The morning number is waiting for you. At 8:52 a.m. the daily P&L reads: net +$1,284, profitable. Revenue $6,420, labor cost 26.1%, food cost 28.4%. The owner knows before the next shift starts — in time to change tonight, instead of reading about it in four weeks.

Why this is the part that matters

Almost every restaurant software product can show you a chart. The hard part has never been the chart — it is getting trustworthy numbers into the system in the first place. Invoice entry is where restaurant data goes to die: it is tedious, it happens late at night, and when it is skipped the food cost percentage on every screen downstream becomes fiction.

Photographing the invoice removes that job. Linking recipes to dishes removes the second one, the nightly count. What is left is a daily P&L that is accurate because nobody had to remember to make it accurate.

The two percentages in the video are the two that decide whether a restaurant survives. Together they are prime cost. If you want the detail behind them, we have written it up properly: what a good food cost percentage is and how to control it, and how to reduce labor cost without cutting your team.
